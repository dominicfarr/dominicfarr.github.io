Part 5 of [How I Hire]({% post_url 2026-10-21-how-i-hire %}). Reflecting: what each hire teaches you about the next one.

{% include how-i-hire-map.md phase=5 %}

Every rule in this series has a story behind it. Hiring too late gave us contractors first. FizzBuzz telling us nothing gave us the shopping-cart kata. The override gave us the unanimous vote. None of it was designed up front. It came from looking back honestly, often, and changing things.

## Then: honest, not measured

At Policy Expert the reflection was qualitative, not quantitative. Every hire gave us feedback on the process, and so did the panel. The take-home and the way we ran it changed quickly as a result. The sales pitch changed too, more naturally, as we learned what good candidates cared about.

Something else happened that we hadn't planned. Word got out that Policy Expert was serious about strong engineering practices, and that brought people to us. Reflection fed sourcing.

## Now: check the hire against the interview

Today I'd add a check at 3, 6 and 12 months. The panel sits down with the interview scores and compares them with how the person is actually doing. Two questions:

1. Is this hire growing?
2. Is the team better for having them?

Where the scores and the reality disagree, the rubric changes. A row that never predicts anything gets rewritten. A level description that keeps misleading the panel gets fixed. That's the loop at the bottom of the series map, from Reflect back to Plan.

Comparing interview scores with later performance is how a selection process gets validated. Most teams never do it, so they never find out whether their interview works.

## The story: the bootcamp grad

One of our hires came out of a bootcamp with nothing much to show on paper. But for interview prep they'd researched Policy Expert's business and its sector, and they came in with details and numbers. Of several candidates, they stood out.

They went on to lead onboarding for several new hires, and did it very well.

Looking back with today's rubric, that interview behaviour is what the mid-level rubric calls "exceeds" for curiosity: "connects their work to a larger system or goal". The rubric didn't pick them. It didn't exist in this form yet.

## Tracking

Then: a spreadsheet, and no applicant tracking system. Low tech for a high-tech organisation. I'd defend that. You don't need to buy a tool to hire well; you need the discipline to write things down.

Now I'd add columns. Pass rates at each stage (CV, first contact, take-home, face to face, offer), time spent in each stage, where each hire came from, how probation went, and the 3-, 6- and 12-month ratings next to the interview scores. That last link is the one that tells you if the process works.

The tracker is a [download](/assets/how-i-hire/hiring-tracker.csv): a CSV that opens in any spreadsheet, with two fictional example rows.

## What I'd do differently today

- Fixed 3-, 6- and 12-month checkpoints, not just feedback when it came up.
- The extra tracker columns.
- The link between interview scores and outcomes, so the rubric gets tested, not just trusted.

## The loop

That's the method: plan early, source with care, vet engineers rather than coders, onboard properly, and look back honestly. Then go round again. Start from the [hub]({% post_url 2026-10-21-how-i-hire %}) or from [Plan]({% post_url 2026-10-21-how-i-hire-plan %}).
