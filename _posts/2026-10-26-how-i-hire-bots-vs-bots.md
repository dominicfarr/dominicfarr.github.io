An afterword to [How I Hire]({% post_url 2026-10-21-how-i-hire %}).

Something has gone wrong in hiring, and AI is speeding it up.

Candidates use AI to write their CVs and send out hundreds of applications. Companies use AI to read those applications and reject most of them before a person ever looks. Bots applying to bots. Each side automates because the other side did, so the volume goes up, the filters get tighter, and fewer people actually meet. It's a war of attrition, and I don't think anyone wins it.

The people who lose are the good candidates who don't fit the pattern, and the teams who never get to meet them.

## Hiring is a human endeavour

Everything in this series rests on judgment about people. Why they made a choice. What level they're really at. Which notes they'd add to the team. None of that survives a keyword match.

Think about who a filter tuned for efficiency throws out. The self-taught engineer. The Geography graduate. The bootcamp grad with nothing on paper who turned up having researched our business. I'd have lost every one of them, and they were some of the best hires I made.

## What the machine is good for

An applicant tracking system is a great tool. Use it for the work that really is mechanical, and keep it away from the judgment.

| Let the software handle | Keep for people |
| --- | --- |
| Location and right to work | Their reasons: why this job, why now |
| Time zone and working hours | Their level, and whether they're ready for this one |
| Fake and duplicate applications | Culture add: what they'd bring that the team lacks |
| Scheduling, reminders, stage tracking | Complementary skills and depth |
| Telling people where they are in the process | Anything that can turn into an -ism: age, gender, background, school, a gap in the CV |

The right-hand column is where the bias hides. A model trained on who you hired before will happily learn who you didn't. Lawmakers have noticed: the EU AI Act [classes AI used to evaluate candidates as high-risk](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), and New York City [requires bias audits](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools) of automated hiring tools.

## The efficiency trap

An automated rejection costs nothing to send, so it gets sent a lot. Every one of those decisions looks cheap. The expensive one is the decision you never knew you made: the great candidate filtered out at the first step. It doesn't show up on any dashboard. You just never meet them, and nobody on the team ever finds out what they'd have brought.

Efficiency is a good goal for the admin. It's a bad goal for judgment.

## Always answer

There are humans on the other side of your machine. Someone spent an evening on that application. Some of them spent a weekend on your take-home. The least they're owed is an answer.

- **Reply when an application arrives.** Say what happens next and roughly when.
- **Reply when someone isn't selected.** Silence is a decision too, and everyone reads it the same way.
- **Give meaningful feedback, if your company allows it.** Centred on the gap, not the person, as in [Onboard]({% post_url 2026-10-24-how-i-hire-onboard %}). A sentence or two that helps them next time is worth more than a polite template.

## Read your values page

Most companies publish their values. Respect, people first, openness, something like that. Read them, then read the rejection email your system sends, or the one it doesn't.

Candidates read both. They can hold you to those values, and they talk to each other. At Policy Expert, word got out that we were serious about engineering, and it brought people to us. Word gets out the other way too.

## Where AI does belong

I'm not against AI in hiring. On my side of the table it can summarise interview notes, with the candidate's consent. It never scores anyone and never rejects anyone. On the candidate's side, I'd rather they used it the way they'd use an IDE, and the face-to-face extension session in [Vet]({% post_url 2026-10-23-how-i-hire-vet %}) shows me whether they understand what it wrote.

Hiring is the most human decision a team makes. Automate the admin. Keep the judgment.
